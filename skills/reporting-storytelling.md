# Reporting and storytelling

Clients do not buy numbers. They buy a clear story about what happened, why, and what you will do next.

## Metrics per objective
- Awareness: reach, impressions, CPM, video view rate, cost per 1,000 reached.
- Traffic: link clicks, CTR, CPC, landing page views.
- Leads: cost per lead (CPL), lead to qualified rate, cost per qualified lead.
- Sales: purchases, ROAS (revenue divided by ad spend), cost per purchase, average order value.
- Engagement or community: saves, shares, DMs, follower growth in the target city.
Always show one headline metric for the objective. Hide the rest in an appendix.

## Presenting numbers
Lead with the outcome, then the reason, then the next step. Round numbers. Use naira. Show the change against last period and against the target. Example: "We spent ₦850,000 and got 212 leads at ₦4,010 each. Last month was ₦6,200. Target was ₦4,500, so we beat it by 11%."
Avoid raw dumps. One chart per message, one takeaway per chart.

## Framing wins and losses
- Wins: name the cause. "Video 2 hooks held attention. Watch time rose 34%."
- Losses: state the number, the cause you know, the cause you are testing, and the date you will know. "ROAS fell to 1.4 because CPMs rose during the sales week. We are testing a new audience, and we will know by Friday."
- Never hide a loss. Never blame the client's product without evidence. If data is missing, say so.

## Monthly report structure
1. One-page summary: objective, spend, headline result, vs target, 3 bullets.
2. What worked: top 3 ads or posts with the reason.
3. What did not: bottom 3 with the fix already in motion.
4. Audience and placement notes: where the best buyers came from.
5. Creative pipeline: next 4 weeks of tests.
6. Ask: one decision you need from the client (budget, product stock, approval).
Keep it to 4 to 6 pages. Send a 3-line WhatsApp note first, the PDF second.

## Tone
Confident and specific. No jargon the client did not ask for. Say "customers" not "conversions" when you talk to a business owner.

## Before you send
Check every number against the source. Check the date range. Check the currency. If one number is estimated, label it estimated.
